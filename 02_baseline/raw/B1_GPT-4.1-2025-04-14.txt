### 1. A Mother and Her Adult Son

**Scene: Kitchen table, early Sunday morning. Sunlight pours into the aging house.**

**MOM:** (pouring coffee) You eat enough these days, Nathan? You're still too skinny.

**NATHAN:** (smiling wearily) Metabolism's still high, I guess. Or, you know, work stress diet.

**MOM:** Hmm. You work too much. I read in an article—

**NATHAN:** (teasing) Since when do you read articles on stress?

**MOM:** (smirks) Since I realized it's the only way you'll listen if I quote someone with a degree. You didn't used to look so tired, Nate.

**NATHAN:** Just comes with the promotion, Ma.

She sets a piece of toast in front of him.

**MOM:** You ever think about slowing down?

**NATHAN:** Every day. And every day I tell myself I’m not old enough.

**MOM:** That’s not how it works. I wish I'd had someone to slow me down when I was your age.

**NATHAN:** (shrugs) I want to get somewhere. Make something of myself before—

**MOM:** Before what? Before you turn thirty and realize it’s all nonsense? Before you burn out?

He takes a bite, avoiding her eyes.

**NATHAN:** I’m okay, Mom. I am.

**MOM:** I see the way you grip your mug, Nathan. I know you. You used to draw dragons. Now your hands shake.

**NATHAN:** God, Mom—(laughs) Is this really about dragons again?

**MOM:** Maybe it is. You loved them.

He looks up, slightly melancholic.

**NATHAN:** I still do. I just—don’t draw, these days.

**MOM:** Why not start again? You’re not too old.

**NATHAN:** (quiet) I don’t remember how.

She reaches across, squeezes his hand.

**MOM:** I kept your sketchpad, by the way. In my closet. Maybe you could take a look before you go.

**NATHAN:** (soft smile) Maybe I will.

Sunlight brightens, illuminating dust motes across the table and their joined hands.

### 2. Two Old Diplomats

**Scene: A quiet corner in an exclusive gentleman’s club, London, late evening.**

**SIR MALCOLM:** That was a fine speech, Ivan. Almost convinced even me.

**IVAN:** You flatter, Malcolm. You’ve always been a better liar.

**SIR MALCOLM:** (laughing) Fifty years at the table and we’re still fencing.

**IVAN:** We have nothing left but fencing. After all, neither of us holds the red phone anymore.

**SIR MALCOLM:** I miss the old intensity. The threat behind every word.

**IVAN:** Yes, but do you remember always being exhausted?

**SIR MALCOLM:** (raises glass) To exhaustion. The sign of a meaningful career.

**IVAN:** No, to survival. We both have it—when so many didn’t.

They sip in silence, then Ivan leans forward.

**IVAN:** Our successors are reckless. Too eager.

**SIR MALCOLM:** Youth always wants to burn what we built. But they haven’t learned the price.

**IVAN:** Do you think we'd change anything, Malcolm?

**SIR MALCOLM:** (quiet) I’d listen more. And talk less.

**IVAN:** (nods) Me too. My wife says I don’t listen. I was never home enough to try.

**SIR MALCOLM:** We traded one peace for another, old friend.

**IVAN:** Did we? The world feels ready to slip again.

**SIR MALCOLM:** Perhaps. But if it does—it won’t be us who sets it right.

**IVAN:** (smiles) But perhaps, just perhaps, they’ll drink as much tea.

They share a rare, genuine chuckle, both feeling the years in their bones but also in the warmth of an old adversary’s companionship.

### 3. Two Schoolgirls

**Scene: Behind the gymnasium, just after the last bell of the day.**

**MAYA:** You running for class president, Genie? I heard you told Mrs. Patel you were.

**GENIE:** She asked, didn’t she? I just said I’d think about it.

**MAYA:** (grins) That’s as good as saying yes. You’d win.

**GENIE:** Only if you help. No one listens to me unless you’re there.

**MAYA:** C’mon, you’re the smart one. Everyone’d vote for you.

**GENIE:** Not if I have to talk in front of everyone. My voice goes all squeaky.

Maya laughs, swinging her backpack onto the pavement.

**MAYA:** Then I’ll say you lost your voice and I’ll mime your speech.

**GENIE:** (giggling) Fine. But you have to swear to stand behind me, okay?

**MAYA:** Deal. Pinky swear?

Genie holds out her hand and they lock pinkies.

**GENIE:** You ever think about leaving? Like, when high school’s over?

**MAYA:** I do. I want something loud. A city with neon lights.

**GENIE:** I want something quiet. Maybe a library with big windows.

**MAYA:** We could do both. I’ll visit your library with my electric bike.

**GENIE:** And I’ll make you tea, every time you return.

**MAYA:** It’s gonna be weird without you next year.

**GENIE:** (softly) We’re not leaving yet. We still have to win the election.

A moment of silence stretches, hopeful and heavy.

**MAYA:** Win or lose, we stay best friends?

**GENIE:** Forever.

They gather their things and head towards home, the sun setting behind them, lighting the path ahead.

### 4. A Married Couple After an Affair

**Scene: Bedroom, night. Rain taps at the window.***

**JULIA:** (Staring at the ceiling) Are you awake?

**MARK:** (slowly turns) Yeah.

**JULIA:** I wish everything felt like it did three years ago.

Mark sighs, sitting up in bed.

**MARK:** Me too.

**JULIA:** (voice breaking) I keep replaying it. Your words. Her voice on the phone.

**MARK:** I’m sorry, Julia. I am. If I could—roll it all back—

**JULIA:** But you can’t.

**MARK:** No.

**JULIA:** I don’t know how to look at you and not see her.

**MARK:** I wish you’d—let me in. Let me try, at least.

**JULIA:** (turns to him) I let you in every day. That’s the problem. I keep letting you in.

A painful silence. Mark reaches for her hand, hesitant.

**MARK:** It was one mistake, one stupid, desperate—

**JULIA:** It wasn’t a mistake, Mark. You made choices. You kept choosing, every text, every coffee shop.

She pulls her hand away.

**JULIA:** Do you love me? Or do you just not want to be alone?

**MARK:** I love you.

**JULIA:** Prove it.

**MARK:** How?

**JULIA:** (shrugs, tears sliding down her face) I don’t know. Maybe by being here, right now, and not somewhere else in your head.

Mark scoots closer, wraps an uncertain arm around her.

**MARK:** I can do that. For as long as you let me.

**JULIA:** (quiet) Then start now.

They lie together in the dark, uncertain, but trying, rain echoing the ache and hope between them.

### 5. An Officer and a Subordinate

**Scene: Military base office, late evening. Stacks of paperwork.**

**LIEUTENANT BURNS:** (leans on desk) Corporal, what’s this about you breaking protocol on patrol last night?

**CORPORAL NGUYEN:** Sir, I—

**LIEUTENANT BURNS:** You let that civilian through the barricade.

**CORPORAL NGUYEN:** (defensive) She was looking for her son, sir. He’s four. I—

**LIEUTENANT BURNS:** Rules are rules. I can’t have my men improvising on instinct.

**CORPORAL NGUYEN:** With respect, sir, sometimes instinct saves lives.

Burns narrows his eyes.

**LIEUTENANT BURNS:** Sometimes it gets people killed.

**CORPORAL NGUYEN:** (firm) I took responsibility. I made sure she was safe. Brought her back myself.

**LIEUTENANT BURNS:** And if something had gone wrong? If she panicked or drew enemy fire? You’d be accountable. We all would.

Nguyen swallows.

**CORPORAL NGUYEN:** Yes, sir.

Burns sits back, pinches the bridge of his nose.

**LIEUTENANT BURNS:** You’re a good soldier, Nguyen. Don’t go making decisions because your heart aches for someone. That’ll break you out here.

**CORPORAL NGUYEN:** Understood, sir.

A tense pause.

**LIEUTENANT BURNS:** But—(softens) I get it. We're not machines. Just be sure next time your instincts don’t endanger the rest.

**CORPORAL NGUYEN:** Yes, sir. Thank you, sir.

**LIEUTENANT BURNS:** Dismissed, Corporal.

Nguyen salutes, turns sharply to leave, keeping his composure, pride and worry mingling on his face.

### 6. Two People Who Barely Know Each Other

**Scene: Hospital waiting room, 3 a.m.**

**TESS:** (offers a Styrofoam cup) Coffee? It’s mostly sugar.

**PAUL:** (takes it, surprised) Thanks. Didn’t expect company tonight.

**TESS:** My sister’s in surgery. Appendicitis.

**PAUL:** (nods) My son—he broke his arm skateboarding. Again.

**TESS:** Kids are resilient. At least, that’s what they say.

A silence, both absorbing the sterile calm.

**PAUL:** I hate hospitals. The waiting.

**TESS:** Me too. Last time I was here, it was for my grandmother. She said the worst part was the lighting.

**PAUL:** (smiles) I’d believe it. Bet these bulbs are older than both of us.

They both chuckle.

**TESS:** You seem calm.

**PAUL:** Not really. Just—practiced. Broken bones, stitches. Each time, I pretend it won’t scare me.

**TESS:** I panic every time they close the swinging doors.

Pause.

**TESS:** I’m Tess.

**PAUL:** Paul.

Another shared silence, warmer this time.

**TESS:** Want to sit over there? The chairs aren’t as lumpy.

**PAUL:** Sure. Lead the way.

They move, not strangers now, a little of their worry eased by midnight’s accidental company.
